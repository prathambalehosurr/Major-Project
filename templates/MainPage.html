About Brain Tumors

A brain tumor is a mass or growth of abnormal cells in the brain. Some are noncancerous (benign) and some are cancerous (malignant). Brain tumors can begin in the brain (primary) or spread from other parts of the body (metastatic). The growth rate and location of a brain tumor determines how it affects the nervous system.

Glioma

Arises from glial cells. Most common and aggressive type of primary brain tumor. Can grow rapidly and infiltrate surrounding brain tissue.

1,426 training samples

Meningioma

Grows on the membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord. Often benign and slow-growing, but can cause serious symptoms due to pressure.

708 training samples

Pituitary

Forms on the pituitary gland at the brain's base. Usually slow-growing. Can affect hormone levels and vision depending on size.

Common Symptoms

Headaches (often worse in the morning)
Seizures or convulsions
Difficulty thinking or speaking
Personality or behavior changes
Weakness or numbness on one side
Loss of balance or dizziness
Vision changes or loss
Loss of hearing
